I want to treat my phone like a gun.

That came to me at my desk today, a few hours into my first work day back from a long weekend offline in the Norwegian mountains. Restlessness and impatience had been building. My hand reached for the phone before any thought arrived. The gesture was automatic. I wasn't going to do anything with it. I just wanted the hit.

## a weekend offline

I'd spent Thursday afternoon to Sunday afternoon staffing an MKP initiation weekend up in the mountains. MKP is the ManKind Project, a men's community. NWTA is its initiation weekend, the New Warrior Training Adventure. The land we used had no cellular coverage.

I knew that going in, so I'd set things up before leaving. Cleared the calendar. Told the people who needed to know that there was an emergency number if anything came up. Put the phone in my bag. I never reached for it during the weekend, because I'd decided not to before I left, and because the weekend itself was busy enough that it wasn't in my mind.

Sunday afternoon we wrapped, and I had one more day in the area before heading home. Phone back on, signal back. Notifications I didn't open. By the time I sat down at my desk this morning, the offline stretch had reset something.

## back at the desk

Today is the first full work day. I'm doing what I usually do, which is software work with two to four coding agents going in parallel. An agent finishes, I check the output, send the next prompt, switch to another. The work is interesting, and it's also a constant slot machine.

I can feel the dopamine climbing, and I notice the baseline moving with it. The cause is right there in the work setup. Two to four agents going at once means there's always one waiting for me to look. The instant the wait starts to feel like a wait, I can switch, and that switch is the hit. To feel anything new by lunch I need a slightly louder hit than the one that worked at breakfast, then a louder one after that. The work is fun. The escalation is the part that reads to me as addiction.

In between all of that, I keep subconsciously picking up the phone. Checking social media. Checking if my love has sent a message. Checking the balance of my bank account. The reach happens before I've decided anything. I just want the hit.

## the gun

I've held a gun a couple of times, at a firing range with someone qualified to teach me. With that came a lot of respect. I didn't gesture with it. I didn't let my hand drift. I kept it pointed down, kept my finger off the trigger, had a clear reason every time I changed the grip. The thing asks for seriousness in a way other objects don't.

The thought I had at my desk was this: I want to handle my phone the same way. Clear purpose before picking it up. Mindful of what I'm doing with it.

The phone won't shoot anyone. But it costs me, in small handfuls of attention I haven't been counting.

The sensation was surreal. I've never felt anything like it before. I've had useful realizations, but this one came in through the body first. Like wisdom landed somewhere lower than language, and I just had to translate it up.

## the practice

So I want to put the phone in a case. Literally and figuratively. Out of casual reach. Not on the desk. Not next to the bed.

I don't know yet how this will hold. I've tried to limit my phone before and lost. The difference this time is the framing. "Limiting" sounds like a diet. "Treat it like a gun" sounds like a stance. I'm hoping the stance carries further.
